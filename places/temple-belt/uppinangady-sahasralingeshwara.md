## The Sangama Lore: "Dakshina Kashi"

Uppinangady is revered as one of the holiest confluence sites in South India. Here, two holy rivers flowing from the Western Ghats—the sacred **Kumaradhara** (originating in Kumara Parvatha) and the **Netravathi** (originating in Kudremukh)—merge together in a sacred embrace (*Sangama*).

According to Skanda Purana, Maharishi Kanva performed severe penance at this river junction. The presiding deity, Lord Sahasralingeshwara, features a unique linga containing 1,000 carved miniature lingas, granting the spiritual merit of visiting a thousand Shiva temples in a single darshan.

During the post-monsoon winter months (November), both rivers flow with gentle, clear currents, creating a serene, breeze-cooled microclimate that relieves car fatigue for children.

- **Safety Rule on River Steps:** **NEVER take an infant or 6yo down to the water edge.** River currents at confluences have hidden undercurrents and slippery moss-covered steps. Enjoy the sacred view from the elevated stone terrace safely.
- **Dress Code:** Modest Indian wear. Men can enter with shirts on in the outer courtyard, but must remove upper clothing to enter the inner sanctum.
- **Family Rest Facilities:** Clean western restrooms are available at *Hotel Aditya* and *Hotel Uppinangady* directly at the NH73 junction (500m away).

## What to Skip
- **River Dip / Snana:** Strictly avoid ritual bathing in the river with young children.
- **Walking down steep muddy riverbanks:** Uneven terrain with infant in arms.
