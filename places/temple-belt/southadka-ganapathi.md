# Southadka Sri Mahaganapathi Kshetra (The Temple of Bells)

**Circuit role:** Highly recommended **child-friendly intermediate temple & rest stop** between Kukke Subrahmanya and Dharmasthala (A unique, tranquil open-air shrine nestled inside a sacred bamboo grove with no enclosing walls or roof, renowned for thousands of hanging brass bells).  
**When to visit:**  
- **Approach A / B / C2 / F:** **Fri 13 Nov 12:15–12:55 PM** (Convenient, serene 35-min halt halfway between Kukke and Dharmasthala).  
- **Approach C1:** **Fri 13 Nov morning**.  
**Driving distance:** ~35 km northwest of Kukke Subrahmanya (~45 min drive); ~18 km south of Dharmasthala (~25 min drive). Located just 3 km off the Kukke-Dharmasthala main road.

## The Open-Air Sanctum Lore

The name *Southadka* translates from Kannada: *Southe* means cucumber and *Adka* means open field. 

Centuries ago, local cowherd boys discovered the ancient Ganesha idol buried in a cucumber field. When villagers attempted to construct a traditional stone temple with granite walls and a gopura, celestial omens and divine dreams instructed them that Lord Ganapathi wished to reside permanently in the open air, with the open sky as his roof, sacred trees as his pillars, and the forest breeze as his cooling fan. 

Devotees whose prayers for children, health, or education are answered return to Southadka to hang a brass bell. Today, thousands of bells—ranging from tiny 2-inch chimes to colossal 50-kg brass temple bells—chime melodiously in the forest breeze.

1. **Why Southadka is Perfect for Traveling Families:**
   - There are **no claustrophobic security lines**, no pushing, and no dress codes requiring shirt removal.
   - The entire area is level and paved, allowing parents to push the 1-year-old in the stroller right up to the sanctum railing.
2. **Bell Chimes & Children's Fascination:**
   - Children of all ages are enchanted by Southadka. Hanging bells of various pitches are accessible along the perimeter walkways, and children are encouraged to touch and ring them.
3. **Feeding & Freshening Up:**
   - The temple serves delicious, freshly prepared *Avalakki Panchakajjaya* prasadam made of pure jaggery, coconut flakes, roasted sesame, and flattened rice. It is mild, naturally energizing, and safe for young children.
   - Quiet stone benches along the bamboo grove provide a private, peaceful spot to feed the baby milk or formula.
