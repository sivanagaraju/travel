## Kids / Baby Logistics & Hazard Evaluation (Why to SKIP for an Infant)

1. **Dangerous Wet Disembarkation:**
   - Small ferries cannot dock on a dry concrete jetty at St. Mary’s. Instead, boats anchor in shallow water.
   - Passengers must climb down a steel ladder into knee-deep, swirling ocean surf carrying their bags. Carrying a 1-year-old infant down a boat ladder into moving seawater is a severe slip-and-fall hazard.
2. **Zero Stroller Mobility & Extreme Heat:**
   - The island has no paved walkways. The ground consists of jagged basalt rocks, coarse broken-shell sand, and uneven boulders.
   - There are virtually no trees or shaded pavilions. Under the midday sun (11:00–15:00), temperatures and rock reflections create intense heat that can quickly dehydrate an infant.
3. **No Baby Infrastructure:**
   - No clean baby-care rooms, no running fresh water taps, and no cafes. If the infant has a diaper blowout or requires warm water for formula, you are stranded until the return ferry departs (often a mandatory 2-hour stay).
4. **Bathing & Swimming Prohibited:**
   - The waters surrounding the basalt columns are deep, rocky, and notorious for dangerous underwater rip currents. Swimming is strictly banned by authorities.

|  - Absolutely no strollers;  - Put phone, wallet, baby  - Take the FIRST |
|    pack a sturdy chest         clothes in dry-bag for     09:00 AM boat; |
|    carrier only.               the wading transfer.       return by 11:30|

- Take the earliest morning ferry (09:00 AM) to avoid scorching midday sun.
- Carry full-brim sunhats, child sunglasses, and mineral sunscreen.
- Bring your own reusable, non-disposable water bottles (single-use plastic is confiscated at Malpe jetty).

## Pleasant Window
- **First Ferry Window (09:00–11:00 AM):** If visiting, only the first morning ferry avoids the blistering midday sun reflecting off volcanic black rocks.
