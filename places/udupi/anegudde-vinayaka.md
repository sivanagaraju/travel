## The Demon Kumbhasura Legend

The name *Anegudde* is derived from *Ane* (Elephant) and *Gudde* (Hillock). 

According to regional tradition, during a severe drought, Sage Agastya performed holy penance here to bring rains to the coast. The cruel demon Kumbhasura terrorized the hermits and attempted to disrupt their prayers. Lord Ganesha bestowed a divine weapon upon the Pandava prince Bheemasena, who slew Kumbhasura on this very hill, restoring peace, rainfall, and fertility to the coastal lands.

1. **Effortless Highway Stopover:**
   - One of the greatest challenges of temple road-trips with infants is long, exhausting detours. Anegudde sits virtually on the highway edge. You lose zero transit time.
2. **Child Blessings & Sanctuary:**
   - Anegudde is considered particularly auspicious for young children. If your 1-year-old infant has not yet had his formal first-year blessing, the priests at the sanctum threshold will readily perform a brief *Ayushya Archana* (prayer for health and long life) and touch the sacred silver flower wand to the baby's head.
3. **Pram Friendly:**
   - Modern accessibility ramps ensure you can push a stroller right up to the footwear drop area.
